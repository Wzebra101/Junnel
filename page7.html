CSS Box Model

Know your space on the floor.

What Is the Box Model?

In CSS, every element is a rectangular box. The box model describes the layers that make up that box, from the content in the middle out to the space around it.

Think of a player on the court. The player is the content, the personal space around the player is the padding, the jersey is the border, and the distance to the next player is the margin.

Box Sizing

By default, width only measures the content. Padding and border get added on top, so the box ends up bigger than you planned. Setting box-sizing: border-box includes the padding and border inside the width, so the box stays the size you asked for.

Total width with content-box is the content width plus the left and right padding plus the left and right border. In this example that is 200 + 40 + 20 = 260 pixels.

Padding and Margin Shorthand

You can set all four sides at once. With four values, the order goes clockwise starting at the top: top, right, bottom, left. It is the same direction a player runs a lap around the court.

padding: 10px 20px 30px 40px;
padding: 10px 20px 30px 40px;

padding: 10px 20px;
padding: 10px;

margin: 0 auto;

With two values, the first is top and bottom and the second is left and right. With one value, all four sides match. The rule margin: 0 auto centers a box with a set width, like a player standing at center court.

Conclusion

Once you can see every element as a box with content, padding, border, and margin, laying out a page becomes much easier. Give each box the right amount of space, and your page will flow like a well-run offense.
