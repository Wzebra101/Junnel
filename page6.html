CSS Background

What Is a Background?

The background is the surface behind an element's content. Think of it as the hardwood under the players, the paint inside the key, or the lights in the arena. CSS lets you set a color, an image, a gradient, or all of them layered together.

In this page, the wooden floor behind the whole site is made with CSS gradients alone, with no image file at all.

The background-color property fills the whole box with one solid color. These are the team colors used across this site.

Background Gradients

A gradient blends colors smoothly and counts as a background image. Use linear-gradient for straight blends, radial-gradient for spotlights, and repeating-linear-gradient for planks of hardwood.

Background Size

The background-size property scales the image. Use cover to fill the box completely, contain to fit the whole image inside, or exact lengths like 20px.

Background Attachment

With background-attachment: fixed, the background stays put while the page scrolls. Scroll up and down and watch the colored stripes in the band below slide behind the text. The hardwood floor behind this whole page uses the same trick.

Layering a Court

You can stack several backgrounds in one rule by separating them with commas. The first layer sits on top. Here a center circle, a half-court line, and wooden planks are layered over a hardwood color.

Multiple backgrounds
background:
  radial-gradient(circle, transparent 0 56px, #fffaf0 56px 60px, transparent 60px),
  linear-gradient(90deg, transparent calc(50% - 2px), #fffaf0 calc(50% - 2px) calc(50% + 2px), transparent calc(50% + 2px)),
  #c98a4b;

Quick Reference

Property Example Value On the Court
background-color #c98a4b The color of the floor
background-image url(ball.png) A logo painted at center court
background-repeat no-repeat One logo, not a hundred
background-size cover Stretch the paint to fill the key
background-position center Where the logo is placed
background-attachment fixed The floor stays while you move

Conclusion

A good background sets the mood before a single word is read, just like a packed arena before tip-off. By combining colors, gradients, images, and positioning, you can build a whole court out of CSS.

Great games start with a great floor.
